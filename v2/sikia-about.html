About Sikia

A legal library built for the students who'll shape Uganda's courts next.

Sikia started in a Makerere lecture theatre. One of us was cited a 1975 decree we couldn't find anywhere online. By the time we'd tracked it down, the lecture had moved on. We kept thinking: this shouldn't be the job.

Why we built it

Finding a case shouldn't take longer than reading it.

Ugandan case law, statutes, statutory instruments, decrees, and legal notices are technically public. In practice they live in photocopied handouts, WhatsApp groups, someone's Google Drive from two cohorts ago, or a PDF a senior hawked you at an overpriced bookstore.

We gathered every current Act. Every major ruling, from the Constitutional Court down. Every statutory instrument and legal notice in force. We indexed them, tagged them by year and area of law, and paired them with curated lecture notes — the kind you actually want to study from the night before the exam.

Then we made it free for students. Because charging a law student for the law itself felt absurd.

What we believe

Three principles. Everything follows from them.

We don't have a mission statement. We have working rules.

01

Free, for students.

No trial. No paywall. No "first three searches free." If you're a Ugandan law student, you get all of it.

02

Current, not comprehensive-for-its-own-sake.

We'd rather have the last ten years done well than fifty years half-done. What's in the library is verified.

03

Built where the work happens.

Kampala, not Silicon Valley. Shaped by law students, not by lawyers who left school twenty years ago.

How we got here

A short, honest timeline.

2023

The PDF that started it.

A lecturer cites Legal Notice 41/2019. Seven of us spend the next hour looking for it. Only one of us finds the right version.

2024

First 200 documents indexed.

A weekend project becomes a Google Sheet. The Google Sheet becomes a prototype. Twelve of our classmates start using it daily.

2025

Four universities. 900 students.

Makerere, KIU, UCU, Uganda Martyrs. Lecturers start asking if they can use it in class.

2026

Sikia Law, public.

We're opening the library to every law student in Uganda. You're reading this page because of that.
